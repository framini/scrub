## Address model

`Sources/ScrubCore/Resources/AddressModel.bin` holds weights Scrub trained itself, with `Tools/AddressModel`, on addresses it generated. No text or list from these sources ships in Scrub, only the weights learned from them:

- **Localities and postcodes:** GeoNames postal code files, one per country (https://download.geonames.org/export/zip/), © GeoNames (https://www.geonames.org), licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 License (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Downloaded on 2 October 2026 for 27 countries. Only the first part of UK, Canadian and Irish postcodes is in these files; the rest of each generated postcode is random.
- **US street names:** U.S. Census Bureau, TIGER/Line Shapefiles 2024, FEATNAMES (https://www2.census.gov/geo/tiger/TIGER2024/FEATNAMES/), for the 110 counties listed in `Tools/AddressModel/tiger-counties.txt`. A work of the U.S. Government, not subject to copyright in the United States (17 U.S.C. § 105).
- **Names** of people in the generated text come from Scrub's name lists (below).
- **Negative examples** include paragraphs of the macOS man pages on the machine that trained it, read locally. None of their text ships or is in the repository.

The street and building words, sentence templates and every number in the generated text are written for Scrub. The short table of cities and postcodes outside the US, Canada, the UK and Australia that address stand-ins are drawn from (`Places.abroad` in `Sources/ScrubCore/Places.swift`) is written by hand from general knowledge.

## Name lists

`Sources/ScrubCore/Resources/NameLists.txt` holds lowercase first names, surnames and ordinary English words. Scrub uses them only as supporting evidence next to another cue (a title, a greeting, a sign-off, a known first name beside a known surname); they never mark a word as a name on their own. `Tools/NameLists/derive.py` writes the file from these sources, each checked against a SHA-256 the script holds:

- **First names:** Social Security Administration, "Baby Names from Social Security Card Applications – National Data" (https://www.ssa.gov/oact/babynames/names.zip). A work of the U.S. Government, not subject to copyright in the United States (17 U.S.C. § 105); data.gov lists it under CC0 1.0 (https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/baby-names-from-social-security-card-applications-national-data). Kept: names given to at least 2,000 people born from 1930 on. The same file's counts by sex also mark which first names are clearly a woman's or a man's: given to at least 200 people born from 1930 on, 95 in 100 of them of one sex. Scrub uses that only to keep a title from joining a first name of the other sex, and to give such a name a stand-in of the same sex.
- **Surnames:** U.S. Census Bureau, "Frequently Occurring Surnames from the 2010 Census" (https://www2.census.gov/topics/genealogy/2010surnames/names.zip). A work of the U.S. Government, not subject to copyright in the United States (17 U.S.C. § 105); data.gov lists it under CC0 1.0 (https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/2010-decennial-census-of-population-and-housing-surnames). Kept: surnames held by at least 1,000 people.
- **Ordinary words:** how often each word is written in lowercase rather than capitalised inside a sentence, counted over 49 public-domain books from Project Gutenberg (listed by number in the script), plus `Tools/ContextModel/common-words.txt`. No text from the books is shipped, only the words that pass the count. A name that is also an ordinary word, or a month or weekday, is marked as such in the file.

Both government sites refuse scripted downloads from some networks; the script then fetches the Internet Archive's copy of the same URL, and the checksum ensures it is the same file.
